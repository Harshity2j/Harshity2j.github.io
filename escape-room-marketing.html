Escape room marketing: make the booking journey work.

By Harshit Pandey · 11 October 2026

Escape room marketing starts with a specific customer decision: who is coming, which room fits the group and when they can book. A campaign needs to answer those questions, not simply attract anyone interested in puzzles.

Separate customer needs

A group of friends, a family and a corporate organiser may need different details. Explain group sizes, duration, accessibility, age guidance and available rooms. Use separate pages or clearly labelled sections when the experience differs.

Make local demand useful

Searches such as “escape room near me” or “escape room in [city]” can suggest local intent, but relevance still needs checking. Target locations your customers can realistically reach and review search terms after launch. These are keyword themes, not a forecast of search volume or bookings.

Give every room a clear page

Show the story without spoilers, practical details, group requirements and how to check availability. Keep the booking action visible on mobile. Do not advertise dates or slots that the business cannot fulfil.

Use Google and Meta for different jobs

Google Ads can reach active search demand. Meta Ads can introduce the experience through suitable imagery and creative. Respect the audience’s experience: useful previews can show the atmosphere without giving away puzzles.

Measure completed bookings

A booking-button click is an intermediate action. Check whether the completed booking and value are recorded, particularly when the customer moves to another booking platform. Deduplicate events and compare campaign reporting with business records.

Plan around available capacity

A sold-out Saturday and an underfilled weekday need different decisions. Review dates, revenue per booking and acquisition costs before increasing spend. Seasonal themes can support the message when they match the real experience.

Questions owners often ask

Do I need a separate page for every room?

Separate pages can help when rooms differ meaningfully. Each page needs useful original information, not repeated text with a new room name.

Should I advertise only at weekends?

Use booking patterns and capacity to decide. Weekdays may need different messaging or group offers, if the business genuinely provides them.

What should I measure first?

Start with confirmed bookings, booking value, acquisition cost and availability. Traffic is supporting evidence, not the final outcome.
